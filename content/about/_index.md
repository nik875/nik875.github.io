I'm Nikhil, a researcher, engineer, and genuine, organic human. I like writing fantasy, going on late-night runs, and dredging up long-forgotten books. I recycle old electronics into my home computing lab to give them a second life. I'm terribly afraid of bees (if one's in my hair, do *not* tell me!). I've devoted my life to AI and its applications, and I believe the final frontier for humanity is human.

By my read, intelligence is not a hard problem. Our biological brains evolved in a world of predators and prey. Intelligence was never guaranteed to defeat stronger jaws or sharper claws. Our sister species were all wiped out, and we ourselves may have experienced [several genetic bottlenecks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_bottleneck#In_humans). Evolution selects for reproductive success, not intelligence. It took millions of years to produce monkeys surpassing the recursive self-improvement threshold, and even then our success was not guaranteed.

**But *we* can select for intelligence in AI.** How much easier could the problem be if AI's objective isn't reproduction? Judging by how far we've come over the past few years, it looks to be much, *much* easier.

I've made a bet with my career that AI will surpass humans in every cognitive task within our lifetimes, probably within 20 years. The period until this intelligence singularity will be filled with human opportunity. For my own goals, I look not toward what AI might automate next, but what problems might come *last*---**our final frontier as a species**.

I expect a "physical Turing test" to be this frontier. If a machine can exist in a physical environment alongside humans, build and maintain long-term social relationships, and successfully operate *us* instead of the other way around, we will truly have become obsolete. This requires closing the physical-world segment of the recursive self-improvement loop and overcoming the uncanny valley, two of the hardest problems in technology that have remained stubbornly unsolved.

Until an embodied AI can tackle these problems, humans will still hold the capital, the concrete mixers, and the social networks. No matter how much better AI gets, someone will need to convince people to put their resources together toward a common goal.

**That is the person I want to be.** My experiences across academic disciplines and private industries serve one core purpose: becoming as effective at organizing humans as possible. I've spent the past decade learning how scientists, engineers, executives, and 9-to-5 employees think. Over the next one, I intend to bring them together to build something that changes the world.
